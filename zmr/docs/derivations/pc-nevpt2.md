# Partially contracted multi-state NEVPT2

Implementation: `zmr/pc/`.  The perturber classes, right-hand sides and
contraction engines are those of UC-NEVPT2 (`uc-nevpt2.md`); only the
first-order space of each external label changes.

## 1. First-order spaces

For a class with external label `e` and active operators `O_X`, the PC
functions are `E_e O_X |Phi_c Psi_I>`.  Two multi-state choices:

* `contraction='union'`: the first-order space of every ket is spanned by the
  functions of all model states.  The span does not change under a unitary
  rotation of the model states, so with equal weights and `e0='average'` the
  effective Hamiltonian is rotation invariant.
* `contraction='state'`: ket `J` uses only `E_e O_X |Phi_c Psi_J>`
  (QD-NEVPT2 of Angeli et al.).  The matrix `a[I, J] = <I|V psi1_J>` is not
  Hermitian, and `H2 = (a + a^+)/2` is used for both E0 choices.

Both coincide for one state.  `V|Psi_J>` lies in the PC space of `J`
(`E_e sum_X v[e, X] O_X |Phi_c Psi_J>`), and functions of different external
labels or classes are orthogonal and `H_D`-orthogonal, so each label is an
independent Hylleraas problem.

Inclusions (same `H_0`, same `E0`): SC space of a label is within the PC
space, which is within the UC space, hence `E_UC <= E_PC <= E_SC` per class.
If the model space is the complete active space, the union contains
`O_X` applied to every CAS state, i.e. the whole target sector, and union PC
coincides with UC (Pr3+ 4f^2 with all 91 states, `validation-log.md`).
The union space contains the state-specific one, so with a common E0 the
diagonal elements satisfy `a_JJ(union) <= a_JJ(state)`.

## 2. Construction from CI vectors

The active parts `f_X^I = O_X |Psi_I>` of the PC functions are CI vectors of
the target sector (dimension `D`).  Collect the `M` chosen ones as the rows of
`Phi` (`M x D`).  The first-order space of the class is found once, for all
external labels, in three steps (`zmr/pc/spectral.py`):

1. **Span.**  The metric `S[X, Y] = <f_X|f_Y>` (`M x M`) and the Gram matrix of
   the determinant coefficients `G = Phi^T conj(Phi)` (`D x D`) have the same
   non-zero eigenvalues; directions with eigenvalue `<= lindep` are discarded.
   The smaller of the two matrices is diagonalized:
   * `M < D`: `S y = s y`, `q = Phi^T conj(y) / sqrt(s)`.  These columns are
     orthonormal only to `eps * cond(S)` (1e-6 for `s = 1e-10`), so one
     Cholesky-QR step `q -> q L^{-+}`, `L L^+ = q^+ q`, follows.
   * `M >= D`: if `G - lindep` is positive definite (one Cholesky
     factorization) the functions span the whole sector; otherwise the
     eigenvectors of `G` above `lindep`.
2. **Koopmans problem.**  `K = Q^+ H_act Q` is diagonalized, `K W = W mu`.
   The cost is one product `H_act Q` and an eigenproblem of the size of the
   span; the sector itself is diagonalized only when the span is the whole
   sector (PC = UC for that class).
3. **Contraction.**  The UC engines run with the orthonormal functions
   `B = Q W` and energies `mu` in place of the sector eigenstates:
   `u^J[k, e] = <b_k| sum_X v[e, X] f_X^J>`, formed either by projecting the
   functions first or, for the three-operator classes, by contracting the
   integrals with the functions first (`zmr/uc/engine.py`).

Every step is level-3 BLAS or a LAPACK driver on matrices of dimension
`min(M, D)` or smaller; no RDM beyond the 1-RDM of the averaged Fock matrix is
needed.

The metric has round-off eigenvalues (about 1e-15) and physical small ones
from nearly full or empty active orbitals (2e-9 for H2O/6-31G CAS(4,4), where
the natural occupations are 2.5e-4 and 0.99939).  Any `lindep` in the gap
gives the same energy; the default is 1e-10.  `lindep` refers to the metric of
the non-redundant strings of `uc-nevpt2.md` section 3.  When the metric
spectrum is continuous down to round-off (many model states whose functions
nearly fill the sector) there is no gap, and the energy depends weakly on
`lindep`: a direction with a small metric eigenvalue has a small overlap with
`V|Psi_J>` but couples to the rest of the first-order space through `H_act`,
so removing it changes the class energy by an amount of the order of
`(E_UC - E_PC)` per removed direction.

## 3. RDM construction

The same quantities follow from transition RDMs:

    S^{IJ}[X, Y] = <I| O_X^+ O_Y |J>
    K^{IJ}[X, Y] = <I| O_X^+ H_act O_Y |J> = <I| O_X^+ [H_act, O_Y] |J> + sum_L S^{IL}[X, Y] h_LJ

The last form holds when the model space is invariant under `H_act` (CASCI
eigenstates or their unitary mixtures; checked at run time).  The formulas
are generated by the Wick engine (`wick.md`,
`generated/pc_nevpt2_terms.md`) for unrestricted operator indices, and the
rows and columns of the non-redundant strings (`uc-nevpt2.md` section 3) are
selected from them.  The functions `c = X W` (with `X` from the
metric eigenvectors above `lindep` and `W` diagonalizing `X^+ K X`) give the
projected amplitudes `T'^I[j, X] = sum_{L,Y} conj(c[(L,Y), j]) S^{LI}[Y, X]`.

The commutator has RDM rank `(|O_X| + |O_Y| + 2) / 2`: three at most, except
for two three-operator strings (the semi-internal blocks of caaa and aaav),
where a 4-RDM contracted with the active two-electron integrals appears.  It
is not built.  Following Chatterjee and Sokolov (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 16,
6343 (2020), appendix), with `O_Y = y_1 y_2 y_3`

    [H_act, O_Y] = sum_k (-1)^(3-k) (O_Y without y_k) [H_act, y_k]
                 + sum_k y_1 .. y_(k-1) [[H_act, y_k], y_(k+1) .. y_3}

(`[ , }`: commutator for an even, anticommutator for an odd number of
operators on the right).  In the first sum `[H_act, y_k]` acts on `|J>`
directly: the intermediate states

    |g^J_a> = [H_act, y(a)] |J> = H_act y(a) |J> - y(a) H_act |J>

are `n` vectors per state (one application of `H_act` each), the two other
operators are applied to them, and the result is projected on the functions
`O_X |I>`: transition quantities with five operators, at the cost of a
3-RDM, `O(N_det n^6)`.  The second sum has at most three operators and gives
3-RDM terms contracted with the integrals.  The Wick engine performs this
split and verifies that no 4-RDM term is left; the explicit 4-RDM formulas
are generated as well and kept in `zmr/reference/pc_rdm4*.py`, where
`test_pc_rdm` compares the two element by element (to 1e-11, also for
vectors that are not eigenstates: the split is an operator identity).

The CI-vector route needs the dense sector Hamiltonians (`D^2` elements) and
matrices of dimension `min(M, D)`; the RDM route needs the 3-RDM (`n^6`
elements per pair of states) and eigenproblems of dimension `M`, which
exceeds `D` for the three-operator classes of a small active space with
several states.  For 4f active spaces with many states the CI-vector route is
the practical one; the RDM route is the form for a few states in a larger
active space and the building block of the internally contracted MRCI.
